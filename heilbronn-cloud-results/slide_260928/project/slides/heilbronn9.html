Certifying the 9-point Heilbronn triangle record

Abbreviations: T = the triangle x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, x + y ≤ 1 · VM = 4-core cloud virtual machine · UTC = Coordinated Universal Time

The problem

Place 9 points in the triangle T so that the smallest triangle any 3 of them form is as large as possible.

Best known set (Cantrell, 2006): smallest area 43/1568, shared by 13 triangles (one shaded). Proved optimal only up to 8 points.

The method

1 · Reduce. An optimal set uses all 3 corners, or has 2 points on one side of T and 1 on each other side.

2 · Model. Maximise the smallest area, with one on/off variable per triangle orientation; the Gurobi solver proves upper bounds.

3 · Split. Fix 5 orientations: 32 parts plus the corners case. A part is proved when its bound is ≤ 43/1568 × (1 + 10⁻⁴).

4 · Refine and check. Split open parts again, 16 pieces at a time. Checks: 7 and 8 points re-certified; a target below the optimum is refused.

Problem. Heilbronn's triangle problem asks how to place n points in a triangle so that the smallest triangle formed by any three of them is as large as possible. We use the triangle T = {x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0, x + y ≤ 1}, whose area is 1/2. For 9 points the best known set is David Cantrell's (2006), on a 1/56 grid, with smallest area exactly 43/1568 (43/784 for a unit-area triangle); 13 of its triangles share that minimum. Optimality is proved only up to 8 points (n = 7 and n = 8 by Sudermann-Merx, arXiv:2607.15021, July 2026, as stated in the project README; the paper could not be opened from the cloud session). Goal. Prove that no 9-point set beats 43/1568 × (1 + 1/10,000) = 0.0274262. With Cantrell's set this would show his set is optimal to within 0.01%, the same standard as the published 8-point result. Method. (1) Every optimal set either uses all three corners, or has two points on one side and one on each of the other two sides; the paper's Proposition 1 says so, and our review found an elementary proof as well. (2) A mixed-integer model maximises the smallest area z, with one binary variable per triangle orientation; Gurobi 13 computes a proven upper bound. (3) Fixing the orientation of triangles (2,3,k), k = 4..8, splits the problem into 32 parts plus the corners case; a part is proved when its bound drops to the target. (4) Unresolved parts are split on four more triangles (16 pieces) and re-run, repeatedly. Checks: 7 points and 8 points re-certified; a negative control with the target just below the 7-point optimum is correctly refused; results are combined with summarize.py plus an independent checker.
